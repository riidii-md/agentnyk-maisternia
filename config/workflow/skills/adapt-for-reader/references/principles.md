# Evidence-Informed Design Principles

Use these principles to resolve tradeoffs. Optimize total reader effort rather
than mechanical brevity.

## Strong defaults

1. **Use signaling for structure and relevance.** Meaningful headings, restrained
   emphasis, and explicit relationships improve attention and learning. A
   103-study meta-analysis found benefits for retention and transfer:
   <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.11.001>.
2. **Adapt support to prior knowledge.** Novices benefit from more guidance;
   experts can be burdened by redundant explanation. See the expertise-reversal
   meta-analysis: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102142>.
3. **Keep related words and visuals together.** Integrated text and diagrams
   reduce split attention: <https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186641>.
4. **Preserve cohesion.** Surface simplicity cannot replace explicit causal,
   referential, and logical connections.
5. **Match representation to task.** Use prose for reasoning, tables for exact
   aligned comparison, and diagrams for spatial, structural, or temporal
   relationships.
6. **Remove decorative competition.** Interesting but irrelevant detail can
   impair learning: <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4>.

## Accessibility floor

For rendered output, preserve semantic structure, sufficient contrast, resize
and reflow, non-justified body text, user-adjustable spacing, and meaningful
text alternatives. WCAG is a minimum compatibility standard, not a guarantee of
comprehension: <https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/#distinguishable>.

## Demote these rules to diagnostics

- A grade-level score does not measure audience knowledge, cohesion, purpose,
  or actionability.
- `7 ± 2` is not a universal maximum for visible document lists.
- Active voice is useful when agency matters, not in every sentence.
- Serif versus sans serif is not a sufficient readability decision.
- More headings, bullets, bold text, diagrams, or whitespace are not always
  better.
- Shorter text is not clearer when it transfers missing inference to the
  reader.

## Verification dimensions

Do not collapse quality into one score. Check:

- time to find the governing message;
- comprehension of claims, relationships, and boundaries;
- correctness of the resulting decision or action;
- evidence and uncertainty visibility;
- confidence calibration;
- perceived and observed effort;
- accessibility in the actual medium.
